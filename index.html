Two-Stage Spur-Gear Speed Reducer

I independently designed a two-stage reducer to transmit 82 hp and step 1,200 rpm down to 100 rpm. I carried the 12:1 requirement through gear sizing, shaft and bearing selection, verification, and a complete SolidWorks assembly.

  • Checked gear bending and contact stress against AGMA criteria; critical contact fatigue FOS: 1.24
  • Sized AISI 1045 cold-drawn steel shafts and ANSI keys; minimum shaft FOS: 1.37
  • Selected SKF bearings from reaction loads and required dynamic ratings; used FEM to check intermediate-shaft deflection and slope

Research / Ongoing independent study

Understanding flow at Mach 6.

Numerical Evaluation of Surface Imperfections in Hypersonic Turbulent Boundary Layers

UTSA · Mechanical EngineeringWork in progress

Building a baseline. Learning to question the results.

I’m investigating hypersonic turbulent boundary-layer flow as part of my ongoing independent study. The broader study examines surface imperfections; this stage focuses on a two-dimensional, adiabatic flat plate using unsteady Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (URANS) simulation.

My work connects OpenFOAM case setup and simulation with ParaView visualization and MATLAB post-processing. I’m grateful for the faculty guidance behind this experience and the opportunity to develop more careful computational and engineering judgment.
